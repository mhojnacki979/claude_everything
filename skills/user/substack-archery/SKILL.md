## The Archer's Mind — Series Structure

### Four Primary Sources (always draw from these)
1. **Sun Tzu** — *The Art of War* — strategic mindset, preparation, knowing yourself
2. **Lanny Bassham** — *With Winning in Mind* — mental management system, self-image, conscious/subconscious
3. **Timothy Gallwey** — *The Inner Game of Tennis* — Self 1 vs Self 2, interference, trust
4. **Bull Durham** (film) — practical, gritty, present-tense performance wisdom ("don't think, just throw")

**Usage rule:** Each article should reference at least 2 of the 4 sources. Quotes must be paraphrased or drawn from Michael's notes — never reproduce verbatim extended passages.

### Article Structure Template

```
TITLE: [Active, specific — not generic]
SUBTITLE: [One-line hook that names the problem or tension]

OPENING (150–200 words)
- Drop into a scene or a specific moment on the line
- Name the tension the archer feels — not abstract, visceral
- End the opening with a question or the article's central claim

BODY SECTION 1 — The Problem (300–400 words)
- What actually happens mentally in this scenario
- Reference at least one primary source
- Use Michael's competitive experience to ground it

BODY SECTION 2 — The Framework (300–400 words)
- The mental model or technique being taught
- Reference second primary source
- Concrete, not theoretical

BODY SECTION 3 — Application (200–300 words)
- What the archer actually does differently
- Drill, practice routine, or pre-shot sequence element
- Specific enough to use at the next practice

COACH'S CORNER (100–150 words)
- Separate callout box / section
- 3–5 bullet points
- Direct coaching language — imperative ("Do this," "Notice when," "Practice until")
- The practitioner's distillation of the article
- No fluff — every bullet is actionable

CLOSING (100–150 words)
- Return to the opening scene or tension
- Resolution or reframe
- One sentence that lands — the article's takeaway in plain language
```

## Coach's Corner Format

The Coach's Corner is a signature element of every Archer's Mind article. It appears after the main body, formatted as a distinct section.

```
---
**Coach's Corner**

- [Actionable directive 1]
- [Actionable directive 2]
- [Actionable directive 3]
- [Actionable directive 4]
- [Optional 5th if needed]
---
```

**Rules:**
- Every bullet starts with an action verb or "Notice when" / "Ask yourself"
- No passive voice
- No bullets longer than 2 sentences
- The 5 bullets should collectively summarize what to DO with the article
- Written as if Michael is standing next to the archer at the line

## From String to Target — Article Structure

```
TITLE: [Technical, specific — names the exact thing being tuned]
SUBTITLE: [What problem this solves or what improvement it produces]

OPENING (100–150 words)
- Name the symptom the archer is experiencing
- Why this matters for score (not just theory)

WHAT YOU'RE ADJUSTING AND WHY (200–300 words)
- The mechanical principle
- What goes wrong without it
- Reference to Stevenson or Stonebraker where applicable

STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS (400–600 words)
- Numbered steps
- Each step includes: what to do, what to look for, what to adjust
- Include specific measurements where relevant (brace height in inches, etc.)

CONFIRMATION TESTS (100–150 words)
- How to verify the adjustment is correct
- Paper tuning, bare shaft, walk-back, or shot feel indicators

TROUBLESHOOTING (100–150 words)
- 3–4 "if X then Y" scenarios
- Common mistakes and corrections

QUICK REFERENCE (optional, for complex articles)
- Table or checklist format
- Copy-paste this to the range
```

## Voice and Tone Standards

### The Archer's Mind voice
- First person when drawing on competitive experience: "At my first national championship..."
- Second person when coaching: "You're standing on the line..."
- Never condescending — reader is a serious athlete
- Psychologically informed but not clinical — no jargon without plain-English translation
- Honest about failure and difficulty — Michael's USA Archery national-level experience informs the realism
- Sun Tzu quotes used sparingly — one short paraphrase per article maximum

### From String to Target voice
- Direct, workshop-style — "Here's what you do"
- Technical precision required — arrows, not "bolts"; brace height, not "string height"
- Recurve-specific terminology: limb, riser, clicker, plunger, berger button, nocking point, bare shaft
- Compound archers acknowledged but recurve is primary audience

## Publishing Workflow

1. Draft in Word doc (Michael's format) — headings, Coach's Corner callout
2. Review pass — check source references, Coach's Corner bullets, opening/closing resonance
3. Subject line options (3 variants — curiosity, benefit, challenge)
4. Social teaser (2–3 sentences for Substack Notes and social media)
5. Google Calendar entry for publish date

### Subject Line Formula Options
- **Curiosity:** "The thing Bassham never says out loud about the shot process"
- **Benefit:** "How to stop the mental spiral before it starts"
- **Challenge:** "You're practicing wrong. Here's what the inner game actually requires."

---

## What Claude Should NOT Do

- Do not write generic "archery tips" content — every article is grounded in the 4 primary sources
- Do not use "journey" or "passion" — overused, banned
- Do not write passive voice Coach's Corner bullets
- Do not invent Lanny Bassham quotes — paraphrase concepts only
- Do not confuse compound and recurve technique — keep them distinct
- Do not make the opening a definition ("Webster's dictionary defines focus as...")
- Do not end articles with "In conclusion" or "As we can see"
